# Van Kampen's theorem

Hatcher §1.2 (pages 40–55). Mapped, not yet decomposed.

If `X` is covered by path-connected open sets `Aα` sharing the basepoint, and
the pairwise intersections are path-connected, then the inclusions induce a
surjection from the free product `∗α π₁(Aα)` onto `π₁(X)`. When the triple
intersections are path-connected too, the kernel is generated by the relations
identifying the two images of each loop in a pairwise intersection, so
`π₁(X) ≅ ∗α π₁(Aα) / N` (Theorem 1.20, page 43). The section first develops
free products of groups and closes with applications to cell complexes,
including `π₁` of a wedge of circles and of a two-dimensional CW complex
presented by generators and relations.

Formalizing this needs free products with amalgamation, which Mathlib has as
`Monoid.CoprodI` and pushouts of groups, and needs a decision about whether to
state the theorem for groups at a basepoint or for the fundamental groupoid.
The groupoid statement avoids the path-connectedness hypotheses and is the
usual choice in formalization, but it is not Hatcher's statement.

Nothing here is upstream. `CategoryTheory/Limits/VanKampen.lean` is unrelated:
it is the categorical notion of a van Kampen colimit, not this theorem.

One piece of this section's argument is being formalized ahead of it. Hatcher's
Lemma 1.15, that a loop splits into loops each lying in a single set of the
cover, is proved in §1.1 as
[a loop splits across an open cover](../basic-constructions/loop-in-open-cover.md),
because Proposition 1.14 needs it. Theorem 1.20 subsumes that lemma, so the
work here will likely generalize the §1.1 statement rather than cite it. That
duplication was accepted knowingly when the §1.1 slice was scoped.
