# Sphere ball pinch

## Public construction

The canonical definition is `Hatcher.Sphere.sphereBallPinch n hn k`, with
`hn : 0 < n`, returning `Hatcher.Sphere.SphereBallPinchData n hn k`.
There is no positive-cardinality premise on `k`.

The geometric definitions in `Hatcher.Sphere` are:

- `sphereBallCenter`: the Euclidean center `4 i e₀`;
- `sphereBallEmbedding`: inverse `puncturedSphereHomeomorphEuclidean`
  followed by the actual subtype inclusion into `TopCat.sphere.{0} n`;
- `sphereBallClosed` and `sphereBallOpen`: images of the closed and open
  Euclidean unit balls at those centers;
- `sphereBallUnion`: the union indexed by `Fin k`.

These sets are defined by their actual formulas. The data records the
last-coordinate omitted point, its zeroth-reflection equation, the open
stereographic embedding, closed disjointness, the closed-ball embedding and
chart facts, and the literal interior equations supplied directly by
`finite_disjoint_sphere_balls`.

The data also contains the actual ball-to-punctured-sphere homeomorphisms and
the homeomorphism from the actual point quotient of the complement of the
union to `Hatcher.PointedWedge` of `Fin k` copies of the same standard sphere,
all based at the same `b`. Its fields specify the collapsed point and each
ball's projection.

The private theorem `sphere_ball_pinch_data_nonempty` constructs this entire
bundle. The public noncomputable constructor chooses its proved witness.
Keeping the certificate in a theorem avoids retaining a large proof-valued
data definition.

## Fixed-point charts and quotient comparison

For each ball, `Topology.IsEmbedding.homeomorphImage` first recovers the
centered Euclidean open-ball subtype. Compose the inverse of
`Homeomorph.unitBall` and the affine
`OpenPartialHomeomorph.unitBallBall` chart to obtain Euclidean space, then use
the inverse of `puncturedSphereHomeomorphEuclidean` to obtain the actual sphere
with `b` deleted.

Compactify the Euclidean chart through
`OnePoint.equivOfIsEmbeddingOfRangeEq`, applied to the same inverse
stereographic embedding with range `{b}ᶜ`. The public finite-point and infinity
equations fix infinity at exactly `b`. The sphere compactness and separation
instances are local transports through `sphereULiftHomeomorph.{0}`.
Openness supplies local compactness of each ball subtype.

The quotient comparison is the composition of:

1. `Hatcher.Relative.compactComplementQuotientOnePointHomeomorph`;
2. the one-point extension of the inverse of
   `Hatcher.Topology.sigmaSubtypeHomeomorphUnion`;
3. `Hatcher.Relative.finiteOnePointSigmaWedgeHomeomorph`;
4. `Hatcher.PointedWedge.homeomorphCongr` for the compactified fixed-point
   charts.

The open balls are disjoint by containment in the constructed disjoint
closed balls. The common omitted point lies in the complement of their union,
including the empty union. Thus the actual compact-complement comparison
applies without assuming a nonempty index type.

The per-ball equation uses the actual compact-complement projection equation,
the disjoint-union inverse's precise sigma-tag equation, the finite sigma
wedge's finite-point equation, and the existing based wedge homeomorphism's
definitional computation. The proof does not name its private helpers.

## Projection and component maps

In namespace `Hatcher.Sphere.SphereBallPinchData`:

- `pinchMap` is the actual point-quotient projection followed by the recorded
  quotient homeomorphism;
- `pinch_map_apply_ball` gives the corresponding wedge inclusion composed
  with the ball's actual punctured-sphere chart;
- `pinch_map_apply_compl` gives the existing wedge basepoint off the union;
- `summandSelector` is the continuous map produced by `PointedWedge.desc`
  from identity on the selected summand and the constant map to `b` on every
  other summand;
- `summand_selector_apply_basepoint` and
  `summand_selector_apply_inclusion` specify its actual values;
- `componentCollapse` is the selector composed with `pinchMap`;
- `component_collapse_apply_ball`, `component_collapse_apply_other`,
  `component_collapse_apply_compl`, and `component_collapse_apply_not_mem`
  give its chart value on the chosen ball and its constant value elsewhere.

The complement equation uses
`Hatcher.Relative.pointQuotientProjection_preimage_point` for the actual
subtype complement pair, without a new quotient model.

At `k = 0`, `pinch_map_zero` identifies the actual empty-wedge projection
with the constant map to its retained basepoint. `emptyWedgeToSphere` is the
actual based continuous map from that wedge, constructed using
`PointedWedge.desc` and empty-index elimination. `emptySphereMap` composes it
with the actual pinch map, and `empty_sphere_map_eq_const` identifies the
result with `ContinuousMap.const` at the same `b`.

No orientation or degree hypothesis is used here. Unit degrees, sign
correction, and signed folding belong to their subsequent leaves.

## Structure elaboration

Elaborating the complete `SphereBallPinchData` structure exceeded the default
`200000` heartbeat limit at weak-head normalization. Its failure to register
the fields then prevented the projection and component declarations from
elaborating. The structure command now has the finite scope
`set_option maxHeartbeats 800000 in`. All original field types are retained;
the scope does not govern the subsequent construction proof or computation
lemmas.

`summandSelector` and `emptyWedgeToSphere` are noncomputable definitions because
their bodies use the actual `TopCat.sphere` object, whose Mathlib definition is
noncomputable. Their mathematical input and output types are unchanged. The
four local class dictionaries in the private proposition proof use anonymous
`let` bindings as preferred by the style linter, with the same proved values.

Focused compiler evidence registers the structure within its finite scope.
The remaining witness-certificate normalization is described below; full
validation still requires the completed build and independent review.

## Closed-ball certificate normalization

The final aggregate `closed_properties` certificate uses the seven facts
already provided by `finite_disjoint_sphere_balls`. Ordinary simplification
left the partially applied function `sphereBallEmbedding n b` in the target,
while the supplied certificate used its defining inverse-stereographic lambda.

The proof now uses builtin `delta` on `sphereBallClosed`, `sphereBallOpen`,
`sphereBallEmbedding`, and `sphereBallCenter` before applying `hballs i`
directly. This explicitly expands the definitions in the target, including
function-valued partial applications. No premise or part of the geometric
certificate is removed, and no declaration or option is added.

The remaining check is the complete witness proof followed by the project's
compilation, trust, full-packet, and independent-review gates.
