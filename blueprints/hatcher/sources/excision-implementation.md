# Singular excision implementation specification

This is a project-authored implementation specification for the small-chain
and excision material in Hatcher §2.1. It is not an independent mathematical
source. The mathematical statements come from the official Hatcher PDF at the
locations recorded in [the section source note](hatcher-2-1.md).

## Mathematical scope

This milestone contains exactly Proposition 2.21 and Theorem 2.20 on printed
pages 119–124, together with the barycentric-subdivision machinery used in
their proofs.

For a family of subsets whose interiors cover a space `X`, let the small
singular chains be the subcomplex generated by singular simplices whose images
lie in a single member of the family. Proposition 2.21 says that its inclusion
into the full singular chain complex is a chain-homotopy equivalence.

Theorem 2.20 has two source-facing forms:

- If the interiors of `A` and `B` cover `X`, inclusion of pairs
  `(B, A ∩ B) → (X, A)` induces an isomorphism on relative homology.
- If `Z ⊆ A ⊆ X` and the closure of `Z` lies in the interior of `A`, inclusion
  `(X ∖ Z, A ∖ Z) → (X, A)` induces an isomorphism on relative homology.

Use the binary-cover form as the primitive excision theorem and derive the
deleted-subset form from it. Both public endpoints must be the canonical maps
induced by inclusions of pairs, not merely unspecified isomorphic objects.

## Representation

Reuse the existing path

`TopPair → Hatcher.Relative.singularPairFunctor → SSetPair`

and the existing relative `chainComplexFunctor` and `homologyFunctor`. Do not
introduce a second `TopPair.toSSetPair` conversion or another relative-chain
quotient API.

For a cover, form the simplicial subset of `TopCat.toSSet X` consisting of
singular simplices whose images lie in a member of the cover. The intended
proof follows Hatcher: construct geometric barycentric subdivision, a chain
homotopy from the identity to subdivision, and the iterated subdivision
operator; use compactness of the standard simplex and a Lebesgue-number
argument to show that every singular simplex becomes small after sufficiently
many subdivisions. Package Proposition 2.21 as a chain-homotopy equivalence.

For a binary cover, identify the relative quotient of the sum of the small
chain complexes with the relative chains of `(B, A ∩ B)`, then transport the
small-chain equivalence to the canonical map into the relative chains of
`(X, A)`. The homology theorem should follow by applying the existing homology
functor to that chain-level homotopy equivalence.

## Intended public contract

The fine roadmap should expose, at minimum:

1. a coefficient-general chain-homotopy equivalence between small singular
   chains for a general cover and all singular chains;
2. a chain-level homotopy equivalence for the canonical binary excision map;
3. an `IsIso` instance for the canonical relative-homology map in the binary
   cover form; and
4. an `IsIso` instance for the canonical relative-homology map in the
   deleted-subset form.

Use the weakest practical category, preadditive, and coproduct hypotheses
supported by the existing singular-chain API at chain level, adding
`CategoryWithHomology` only for the homology endpoints. Hatcher's integral
theorem is the specialization to `AddCommGrpCat.of ℤ`. Naturality should be
inherited from the canonical functorial maps; this milestone does not require
a separate naturality theorem.

## Coarse implementation phases

This specification deliberately fixes only four phases. Fine theorem leaves
and their dependency edges will be added only after the coarse contract is
approved.

1. affine-simplex and singular-subdivision foundations;
2. the small-simplices chain equivalence of Proposition 2.21;
3. relative quotient dévissage and binary excision at chain level; and
4. the cover and deleted-subset homology forms of Theorem 2.20.

## Pinned Mathlib boundary and prior art

The repository pins Mathlib `v4.34.1` at
`d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612`. That pin provides abstract
simplicial-set subdivision (`SSet.sd`), `SSetPair` relative homology, and the
generic tools sending chain-homotopy equivalences through quasi-isomorphisms
to homology isomorphisms. Its abstract subdivision is distinct from the
geometric singular subdivision needed here. The pin does not provide that
singular subdivision operator, the small-singular-chain equivalence, a
topological excision condition, or the singular excision theorem required by
this milestone. In particular, derive the public homology endpoints through
the pinned `HomotopyEquiv.toHomologyIso` or
`HomologicalComplex.homotopyEquivalences_le_quasiIso` machinery rather than
attributing homology-level excision declarations to the prior-art repository.

Joël Riou's
[`excision`](https://github.com/joelriou/excision/tree/8b56cd0c8e5f39a7c2f36418c80b298e469596a6)
development at commit `8b56cd0c8e5f39a7c2f36418c80b298e469596a6` is the primary
implementation prior art. Its relevant interfaces include
`TopCat.toSSet.subcomplexOfSets`,
`TopCat.SmallSimplicesCondition.exists_sdIterIsSmall`,
`TopCat.SmallSimplicesCondition.homotopyEquiv`,
`TopCat.SmallSimplicesCondition.homotopyEquivalences`,
`TopCat.singularChainComplexSd`,
`TopCat.singularChainComplexHomotopyIdSd`,
`SSetPair.homotopyEquivalence_chainComplexMap_iff_of_mono`,
`TopCat.ExcisionCondition.homotopyEquivalences`, and
`TopPair.homotopyEquivalences_of_excision`.

This repository snapshot is neither the mathematical source nor pinned
Mathlib coverage.
The affine-map cone support from Mathlib PR
[#43524](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/43524) merged to
master after the `v4.34` stable branch point and is therefore absent from this
`v4.34.1` pin, while PR
[#43528](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/43528) for affine
simplices remains open. The remainder of the excision development has not been
merged upstream. No node based on it may carry `mathlib: true`.

## Explicit exclusions

This milestone does not include Proposition 2.22, the good-pair quotient
comparison, Theorem 2.13, sphere applications, Mayer–Vietoris, the
simplicial–singular comparison, or any result from §2.2. Those are downstream
milestones even when excision is one of their prerequisites.

## Choices to resolve during fine decomposition

- Prefer a general-family small-simplices engine over a binary-only duplicate,
  while keeping the public excision theorem binary.
- Choose collision-resistant local declaration names until upstream naming is
  stable.
- Decide whether to backport the post-pin affine cone helpers or express the
  same construction using only the pinned API.
- Fix the subset-inclusion constructors so the public excision morphisms are
  definitionally the maps produced by the existing relative-homology functor.
- Confirm the weakest usable coefficient hypotheses before fixing leaf
  statements.
- Expose the chain-homotopy-equivalence property publicly, while keeping
  subdivision retractions and auxiliary homotopies private unless downstream
  reuse justifies separate declarations.
- Keep the local layer replaceable by future Mathlib excision APIs; prior-art
  alignment is not permission to claim upstream provenance.
